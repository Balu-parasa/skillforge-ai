# Design Decisions — SkillForge AI

## Why did I choose this approach?

I chose to build SkillForge AI as a premium, product-led career growth experience rather than a traditional marketing landing page.

The main idea is simple: a student chooses a target career, understands their skill gaps, and gets a clearer direction on what to learn or build next.

I focused the experience around the product itself so that the interface demonstrates the concept instead of relying only on marketing copy.

I used React with reusable components so the product preview and interactive role experience could be kept consistent and easy to iterate.

## What trade-off did I make because of time?

Because this was a time-boxed assessment, I focused on the core landing-page experience instead of building a complete backend, authentication system, or production AI pipeline.

I prioritized:

- the first-screen experience
- product visualization
- meaningful interaction
- responsive behavior
- animation and micro-interactions
- visual polish

The dashboard uses clearly identified sample data rather than pretending to analyze real users.

## Where did you use AI?

I used AI coding and design tools during the development process for brainstorming, UI/UX exploration, implementation assistance, debugging, and refinement.

I reviewed and adapted the generated implementation rather than treating the generated output as a finished product.

I tested the final interactions, responsive layouts, production build, and code quality, and made the final decisions about the product experience and design direction.

The final implementation was intentionally kept understandable so that I can explain the major components, interactions, trade-offs, and design decisions during the follow-up interview.
